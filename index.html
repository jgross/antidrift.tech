Five things we believe

  1. One session, one goal.

    A session that can't say what it's for isn't working. It's wandering. Every session in Antidrift belongs to a goal, and the goal has a "done when".

  2. Agents don't get tired. You do.

    Babysitting sixteen tabs is not a job. Antidrift watches every session and puts only the few that need you on one board: drifted, waiting on you, or probably done.

  3. "Done" means done.

    "I've implemented the feature!" is a claim, not a result. Goals carry done-when criteria that you tick off, and when the last one is ticked the board asks you to close the goal.

  4. Your Mac. Your code. Your business.

    No account. No cloud. No telemetry. Antidrift keeps everything in a SQLite file on your disk. The one optional extra, a board assistant, calls Claude with your own API key and asks before it changes anything.

  5. Quit the app, keep the work.

    Every session lives in its own tmux server. Quit Antidrift, update it, crash it: your agents keep going. Attach from any terminal you like.

Fair questions

How does it know a session drifted?
It compares the words in your prompts with the words in the session's goal and its done-when criteria. Three prompts in a row with nothing in common, and the session is drifting. It runs on your Mac: no LLM, no tokens, no cost per session. Click "It's fine" and those words join the goal.
Do I have to start my sessions inside it?
No. Antidrift adds hooks to Claude Code, so it sees every session, whether it started in Antidrift, in Terminal, iTerm or your editor, in any folder or worktree. Sessions you open in Antidrift also get a tab and survive the app quitting.
Who decides a goal is done?
You. Criteria are checkboxes. Antidrift won't pretend the tests passed; it puts the goal in front of you when every box is ticked.
What do I need?
A Mac, Claude Code, and python3 for the hooks (most dev Macs have it). tmux comes inside the app.
